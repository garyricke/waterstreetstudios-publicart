In Batavia · River Street Plaza

Batavia’s newest seats are made from retired wind turbine blades.

The unWIND project commissions three Illinois artists to paint sculptural benches built from upcycled turbine blades for the City’s new River Street Plaza. It’s a fitting gesture for the Windmill City.

Public Art Initiative

At the turn of the 20th century, Batavia was the world’s windmill manufacturing capital, which is how it earned the name the Windmill City. More than a hundred years later, wind power is coming back to downtown in a different form: as places to sit.

The unWIND River Street Plaza Benches Project brings three sculptural benches, made by Noblewins from retired wind turbine blades, to the City’s new River Street Plaza at 102 N. River Street. Water Street Studios’ Public Art Initiative, working with the City of Batavia, is commissioning three artists to paint them: two Arch benches and one Arcadia bench. Each one becomes a permanent piece of functional public art.

A bench that started as a blade

Retired turbine blades are a tough industrial waste stream: they’re built to survive decades of weather, which also makes them hard to get rid of. Noblewins turns that durability into an advantage. Each bench pairs the blade form with upcycled PVC board that looks like natural wood without warping or fading, a rust-resistant aluminum frame and 316 stainless steel fasteners. It’s backed by a 25-year warranty.

The Arch is a round portal with a long seat threaded through it. The Arcadia is taller: two blades sweep up and meet overhead in a sail shape, with the bench between them. It’s meant to be the sculptural anchor of the plaza.

“These projects are about more than art, they’re about storytelling.”

Bonnie Baumgartner · Co-chair, Water Street Studios Public Art Initiative

Connection through community

The theme for all three benches is Connection Through Community. The call for artists asked for designs that reflect the beauty, diversity and spirit of downtown Batavia and invite people to stop, gather and connect. It also asked for work that complements what’s already on the site: the plaza’s metal tree ironwork and its copper-toned, leaf-patterned privacy fencing.

The call went out on April 29, 2026, alongside the call for the America 250 mural at City Hall. Both were open to Illinois-based artists and teams, emerging or established. Bench artists were offered contracts of up to $3,200 each to finish their designs and paint on site at the plaza. Proposals were due May 8.

“We’re looking for artists who can help capture the spirit of Batavia and create something that residents and visitors will connect with for years to come.”

Sabrina Martin · Co-chair, Water Street Studios Public Art Initiative

A new room for downtown

River Street Plaza is the City’s new downtown gathering space, built to host events like the Batavia Farmers Market and to strengthen the River Street corridor. The City’s master plan sets the benches among brick paving, tables and group seating, a native pollinator garden, a limestone retaining wall, catenary lighting, bike racks with a repair station, and a restroom building with a drinking fountain.

The project got noticed early. After the April 29 media release, WBBM Newsradio 780 interviewed Bonnie Baumgartner for an on-air segment. Patch and the Batavia Chamber of Commerce both carried the announcement.

Why it matters

Public art you can sit on.

Most of the Water Street Public Art Initiative’s work so far has gone on walls. The unWIND benches are different: they’re art people use every day. Someone eating lunch, a family at the Farmers Market or a cyclist stopping at the repair station will all end up sitting on a local artist’s work.

They also tie Batavia’s story together. The town that built windmills for the world now seats its residents on retired wind turbine blades, steps from the Fox River that powered its mills. Like the America at 250 mural at City Hall, the benches tell Batavia’s history in the places where people already spend their time.
